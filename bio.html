About Aafaque R. Khan

Astrophysicist and technologist specializing in ultraviolet instrumentation, detector development, and space-based telescopes. Doctoral Candidate at Steward Observatory, University of Arizona, and NASA Future Investigator (FINESST Grant). Lead engineer for Solar Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope (SUIT) on ISRO's Aditya-L1 mission. Experience includes NASA Aspera SmallSat, FIREBall-2, and SuperLOTIS projects. Expertise in optical engineering, detector characterization, and collaborative research with international teams. Published in SPIE, AAS, and ISRO symposiums.

International Collaborations

My work spans institutions across two continents. At IUCAA, Pune (2015–2019), I led cross-functional engineering teams across multiple ISRO centers to deliver India's first solar UV space telescope. Since moving to the University of Arizona, I have collaborated with JPL/Caltech on delta-doped detector development, the Centre for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian during a pre-doctoral fellowship (2022–2024), and Laboratoire d'Astrophysique de Marseille on the FIREBall-2 stratospheric balloon mission.

Beyond the Lab

I serve as a mentor for undergraduate and early-career researchers through the Hamden Lab mentoring program. I am also active in science communication and public outreach — see my Outreach page for initiatives including telescope nights, school visits, and astronomy education efforts. In 2012, I was selected as an Emerging Space Leaders awardee by the International Astronautical Federation and served as a panelist at IAC Naples, moderated by Bill Nye.

Aspera SmallSat

NASA Astrophysics Pioneers mission mapping warm-hot gas in the circumgalactic medium. Leading optical integration and calibration of twin far-UV spectrographs.

FIREBall-2

Stratospheric balloon-borne 1-m NUV telescope with a multi-object spectrograph for studying CGM emission from nearby galaxies.

UV Detector R&D

Characterizing EMCCDs and Skipper CCDs for photon-counting UV astronomy. Developing ALD anti-reflection coatings for next-generation space missions.
