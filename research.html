Research

The focus of my research is to develop new detector technologies and instruments to enable breakthrough discoveries in astrophysics. I specialize in developing space-based imaging and spectroscopy instruments operating in the ultraviolet.

My research sits at the intersection of ultraviolet detector physics, precision optical engineering, and space mission development. I build and characterize next-generation photon-counting detectors and design the optical systems that enable them to observe the faint UV emission from gas surrounding galaxies.

UV Detector Development

Silicon-based detectors — EMCCDs, Skipper CCDs, and delta-doped arrays — are among the most sensitive UV light sensors available, but their performance is limited by dark current, charge transfer inefficiency, and quantum efficiency losses at short wavelengths. My research characterizes these noise mechanisms at the single-photon level, develops atomic-layer-deposited (ALD) anti-reflection coatings, and tests novel detector architectures to push sensitivity into the far ultraviolet.

This work is carried out in collaboration with JPL/Caltech and feeds directly into flight-ready detector systems for NASA missions.

Space Instrumentation

I apply detector expertise to real mission hardware. As Co-Investigator on NASA's Aspera SmallSat, I lead optical integration, alignment, and calibration of twin far-UV spectrographs designed to map warm-hot circumgalactic gas. Earlier, as Lead Engineer for ISRO's Aditya-L1 SUIT, I delivered India's first solar UV space telescope from concept through Critical Design Review to successful launch in September 2023. I also contributed to the FIREBall-2 stratospheric balloon mission and the NUTRANS UV transient detection program.
